### 1) Dostoevsky
When it comes to **Dostoevsky**, the thing I love most is that nobody in his books is just a "good guy" or a "bad guy." Every single character is this messy, chaotic blend of both. They feel like actual people you’d meet in real life, which is kind of scary when you think about the stuff they do.

He has this incredible way of exploring massive, brain-melting ideas through his characters. Instead of just telling you what he thinks, he gives different opinions to different people and lets them live those ideas out.

One character might be a complete nihilist while another is deeply spiritual, and the best part is that Dostoevsky makes both of them sound totally convincing. You get to see exactly how they arrived at their conclusions and the "why" behind their choices. It’s like you’re living through five different perspectives at once.

It makes the reading experience feel less like a lecture and more like a debate that’s happening inside your own head. You start to understand characters you’d normally hate because you’ve seen the world through their eyes for three hundred pages. It’s heavy stuff, but it makes every other "hero vs villain" story feel a bit flat by comparison.
