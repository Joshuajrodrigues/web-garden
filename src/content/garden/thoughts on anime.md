So, like a lot of people, I got into anime through Dragon Ball Z. Back then, it aired at 10 PM, and I had to fight for my life to make excuses to my parents just to stay up a few more seconds to catch the episodes. They just saw it as "cartoons," but there’s a massive difference between Looney Tunes and something like Naruto. Honestly, anime is more like Indian drama serials—the same intensity and style, but with a much higher quality and standard.

What’s interesting is how anime—from Shonen to Seinen and even comedies—protects these "unrealistic" ideals that the adult world tries to dilute. As we grow up, society tries to make us cynical. It tells us to be "realistic," which is usually just code for being defeated. But anime refuses to comply.

In Shonen, it’s about the audacity to dream and the belief that effort actually matters. In Seinen like Monster, you have characters like Tenma who refuse to let go of the ideal that every life has value, even when the world is a literal nightmare. Even Akira shows that struggle for agency when a broken system tries to crush you.

Then there’s Gintama. It uses humor as a shield. Gintoki is an adult who has lost everything, yet he uses jokes to protect his soul and his own "inner bushido." It shows that even when you’re broke and the world is a mess, you don’t have to become a cynical cog in the machine.

Whether it’s a kid yelling about destiny or a hungover guy with a wooden sword, these stories show that it’s worth it to hold on to your spirit. Even if the full world says no, staying "naive" enough to believe in something is exactly what keeps you human.
